Phase 4 Historical Backtest Lab

A preliminary monthly point-in-time test of the same transparent score. It uses only annual SEC facts filed by each historical test date and price information available through that date. The top five are tested because the prototype universe currently contains only 12 companies.

What is a backtest?

A backtest asks: “What would this system have selected using only information available at that past date?” It can reveal strengths and weaknesses, but it cannot perfectly reproduce real investing and may be affected by the small company universe and survivorship bias.

Phase 6 Past–Present–Future Outlook

How to read operating scenarios

The conservative, base, and optimistic paths show different possible business outcomes. They project revenue and free cash flow—not the future stock price. The purpose is to understand what assumptions would need to be true under different conditions.

Phase 7 Capital Efficiency & Financial Durability

Compares how each company funds reported CapEx and R&D, converts operating cash flow into free cash flow, and carries cash versus debt. The score is peer-relative within the current AI subsector universe and does not identify AI-only spending.

Capital-efficiency basics

CapEx is spending on long-lived assets such as equipment and data centers. R&D is research and product-development expense. Free cash flow is operating cash flow minus cash capital expenditures. Strong efficiency generally means a company can fund growth without excessive financial strain.

Phase 8 Relative Valuation & Growth Quality

Compares market capitalization and enterprise value with reported revenue, earnings, and standardized free cash flow. Lower multiples are considered together with growth and cash-flow quality; this is not an intrinsic-value estimate or price target.

Valuation basics

Valuation asks how much investors currently pay for each dollar of sales, earnings, or free cash flow. A lower multiple is not automatically better, because faster-growing or stronger companies may deserve a premium. Phase 8 compares valuation with both peers and growth quality.

Phase 9 SEC Filing Catalyst & Reaction Monitor

Classifies recent SEC filings using form types and disclosed 8-K item codes, then measures the observed first-close and five-trading-day stock reactions. This is a disclosure and reaction monitor—not news sentiment, causation analysis, or a prediction.

How to read filing reactions

An SEC filing may coincide with a market move, but the filing may not be the only cause. The one-day and five-day figures are observations around the filing date, not proof of causation and not forecasts of the next move.

What to examine in a company profile

Start with reported revenue, free cash flow, cash, debt, and data coverage. Then review the score components, peer table, supporting evidence, risks, scenarios, valuation, and recent filings. No single measurement should decide an investment.

How the preliminary score works

Revenue growth 25% · standardized free-cash-flow strength 20% · financial strength 15% · profitability 10% · market momentum 20% · R&D and capital-investment intensity 10%. Missing inputs are excluded and the remaining weights are renormalized. The score ranks financial and market characteristics. Phase 5 maps broad score bands to a conservative, backtest-based 12-month peer-outperformance research estimate using earlier observations for calibration and later observations for validation.
