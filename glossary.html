Glossary

Every indicator, index, and rate used across Stock Digest - what it is, what it measures, and who calculates it.

1-Year Treasury

Yield on a US government bond maturing in 1 year.

Reflects market expectations for Fed policy over the coming year.

Calculated and published daily by the Federal Reserve (H.15 release).

10-Year Treasury

Yield on a US government bond maturing in 10 years.

The benchmark long-term rate that heavily influences mortgage rates and stock valuations.

Calculated and published daily by the Federal Reserve (H.15 release).

10-Yr minus 2-Yr Spread

The 10-year Treasury yield minus the 2-year Treasury yield.

A negative (inverted) spread has historically preceded US recessions, since it means short-term rates exceed long-term rates.

Calculated from Treasury yields tracked by the Federal Reserve (H.15 release).

10-Yr minus 3-Mo Spread

The 10-year Treasury yield minus the 3-month Treasury yield.

This version of the yield curve is the one the Federal Reserve's own research has cited as its preferred recession-signal spread.

Calculated from Treasury yields tracked by the Federal Reserve (H.15 release).

10-Yr Treasury

The yield on a 10-year US government bond.

It's the benchmark long-term interest rate that drives mortgage rates and stock valuations across the economy.

Yields are set by Treasury auctions and tracked daily by the Federal Reserve (H.15 release).

2-Year Treasury

Yield on a US government bond maturing in 2 years.

A widely watched gauge of near/medium-term Fed policy expectations.

Calculated and published daily by the Federal Reserve (H.15 release).

2-Yr Treasury

The yield on a 2-year US government bond.

It reflects where bond markets expect the Fed to set interest rate policy over the next couple of years.

Yields are set by Treasury auctions and tracked daily by the Federal Reserve (H.15 release).

3-Month Treasury

Yield on a US government bond maturing in 3 months.

Reflects near-term interest rate expectations and is closely tied to the Fed's current policy rate.

Calculated and published daily by the Federal Reserve (H.15 release).

30-Year Treasury

Yield on a US government bond maturing in 30 years.

The longest-dated widely tracked Treasury yield, reflecting very long-term growth and inflation expectations.

Calculated and published daily by the Federal Reserve (H.15 release).

30-Yr Mortgage

The average interest rate on a 30-year fixed-rate US home loan.

It measures the typical borrowing cost for a conventional home purchase.

Published weekly by Freddie Mac's Primary Mortgage Market Survey.

30-Yr Mortgage Rate

The average interest rate on a 30-year fixed-rate US home loan.

It measures the typical borrowing cost for a conventional home purchase.

Published weekly by Freddie Mac's Primary Mortgage Market Survey.

5-Year Treasury

Yield on a US government bond maturing in 5 years.

Bridges short-term policy expectations and longer-term growth/inflation expectations.

Calculated and published daily by the Federal Reserve (H.15 release).

Aaa Corporate Bond Yield

The average yield on US corporate bonds rated Aaa by Moody's - the highest possible credit rating, indicating the lowest default risk among corporate borrowers.

A benchmark for the cheapest corporate borrowing costs available.

Calculated by Moody's; tracked via the Federal Reserve's FRED database.

Aaa Spread over 10-Yr Treasury

The extra yield Aaa-rated corporate bonds pay over the 10-Year Treasury.

Measures the minimum credit risk premium investors demand even for the safest corporate borrowers.

Calculated from Moody's and Federal Reserve data, tracked via FRED.

AEX (Netherlands)

The Netherlands' benchmark index of the largest companies on Euronext Amsterdam.

Tracks the health of the Netherlands' largest public companies.

Calculated and maintained by Euronext.

ASX 200 (Australia)

Australia's benchmark index of the 200 largest companies on the Australian Securities Exchange.

Tracks the health of Australia's largest public companies, heavily weighted toward mining and banks.

Calculated and maintained by S&P Dow Jones Indices.

ATX (Austria)

Austria's benchmark index tracking the largest companies on the Vienna Stock Exchange.

Tracks the health of Austria's largest public companies.

Calculated and maintained by Wiener Börse (the Vienna Stock Exchange).

Australian Dollar

The official currency of Australia.

Exchange rate shown here is against the US dollar.

Published daily by the Federal Reserve (H.10 foreign exchange release).

Avalanche

A blockchain platform designed for fast transaction finality and high throughput, used for decentralized finance and custom blockchain deployments.

Its native token (AVAX) is used to pay network fees.

Price tracked via major exchanges through Yahoo Finance.

Baa Corporate Bond Yield

The average yield on US corporate bonds rated Baa by Moody's - the lowest investment-grade rating, one step above 'junk' status.

Widely used as a gauge of borrowing costs for more moderately-rated companies.

Calculated by Moody's; tracked via the Federal Reserve's FRED database.

Baa Spread over 10-Yr Treasury

The extra yield Baa-rated corporate bonds pay over the 10-Year Treasury.

A widely watched gauge of overall corporate credit risk appetite - a widening spread often signals rising recession or default fears.

Calculated from Moody's and Federal Reserve data, tracked via FRED.

BEL 20 (Belgium)

Belgium's benchmark index of the 20 largest companies on Euronext Brussels.

Tracks the health of Belgium's largest public companies.

Calculated and maintained by Euronext.

Bitcoin

The original and largest cryptocurrency by market value.

A decentralized digital currency not issued by any government or central bank, often described as 'digital gold' due to its capped 21 million coin supply.

Tracked via public blockchain data; price shown here reflects trading on major exchanges via Yahoo Finance.

Bitcoin Cash

A cryptocurrency created in 2017 when a group of Bitcoin developers disagreed over how to scale the network and split off ('forked') their own blockchain with larger block sizes.

Designed as a more payments-focused alternative to Bitcoin.

Price tracked via major exchanges through Yahoo Finance.

Permits issued for future home construction, annualized in thousands of units.

A leading indicator for housing starts, since permits are typically pulled before construction begins.

Calculated and published monthly by the U.S. Census Bureau, jointly with HUD.

Cardano

A blockchain platform for smart contracts and decentralized applications, notable for its academic, peer-reviewed approach to development.

Its native token (ADA) is used to pay transaction fees on the network.

Price tracked via major exchanges through Yahoo Finance.

Chainlink

A network that supplies external, real-world data (like asset prices) to blockchains, which can't natively access outside information on their own.

Its native token (LINK) is used to pay for these data services.

Price tracked via major exchanges through Yahoo Finance.

Chinese Yuan

The official currency of mainland China, also called the renminbi.

Its exchange rate is managed within a band set by China's central bank, rather than freely floating like most other currencies on this page.

Published daily by the Federal Reserve (H.10 foreign exchange release).

Cocoa

Cocoa, price per metric ton.

The raw input for chocolate production, with supply concentrated in a small number of West African countries, making it prone to sharp price swings.

Priced and traded on ICE (Intercontinental Exchange).

Coffee

Coffee (Arabica), price per pound.

A tropical soft commodity heavily influenced by weather in major growing regions like Brazil and Vietnam.

Priced and traded on ICE (Intercontinental Exchange).

Copper

Copper, price per pound.

Nicknamed 'Dr. Copper' because demand tracks construction and manufacturing activity closely, making it a widely watched economic health gauge.

Priced and traded on the COMEX exchange, operated by CME Group.

Core PCE Inflation (YoY)

Core Personal Consumption Expenditures Price Index, year-over-year change.

It measures inflation in consumer spending but excludes food and energy, since those categories are volatile and can distort the underlying trend.

Calculated and published monthly by the Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA), and it's the Federal Reserve's own preferred inflation gauge for setting monetary policy.

Corn

Corn, price per bushel.

A major agricultural commodity used for food, animal feed, and ethanol - prices are sensitive to weather, planting reports, and biofuel demand.

Priced and traded on the CBOT (Chicago Board of Trade), part of CME Group.

Cotton

Cotton, price per pound.

A key input for the textile industry, sensitive to both agricultural conditions and global apparel demand.

Priced and traded on ICE (Intercontinental Exchange).

CPI (Inflation)

The Consumer Price Index, year-over-year change.

It measures how much the prices consumers actually pay for a broad basket of goods and services rose over the past 12 months - the most widely cited inflation gauge.

Calculated and published monthly by the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS).

CPI Inflation (YoY)

The Consumer Price Index, year-over-year change.

It measures how much prices consumers actually pay for a broad basket of goods and services rose over the past 12 months - the most widely cited inflation gauge, and the one compared here to the Fed's 2% target.

Calculated and published monthly by the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS).

Credit Card Balances

Total outstanding credit card debt held by US consumers.

A subset of total consumer credit specifically covering revolving debt, which typically carries the highest average interest rates.

Calculated and published by the Federal Reserve, tracked via FRED.

Credit Card Delinquency Rate

The share of credit card balances 30+ days past due.

A leading indicator of consumer financial stress - rising delinquencies often precede pullbacks in consumer spending or tighter lending standards.

Calculated and published quarterly by the Federal Reserve.

Danish Krone

The official currency of Denmark, pegged to the Euro within a narrow band as part of the European Exchange Rate Mechanism.

Exchange rate shown here is against the US dollar.

Published daily by the Federal Reserve (H.10 foreign exchange release).

DAX (Germany)

Germany's blue-chip index of 40 major companies listed on the Frankfurt Stock Exchange.

Tracks the health of Germany's largest public companies - Europe's biggest economy.

Calculated and maintained by Deutsche Börse (via its STOXX subsidiary).

Dogecoin

Originally created in 2013 as a joke based on the 'Doge' internet meme.

It has since become one of the largest cryptocurrencies by market value despite having no specific technical purpose beyond simple payments.

Price tracked via major exchanges through Yahoo Finance.

Dow Futures (E-mini)

A futures contract tracking the Dow Jones Industrial Average, tradable nearly 24 hours a day.

Since it trades outside normal stock market hours, it shows where traders expect the Dow to open.

Traded on the CBOT (Chicago Board of Trade), part of CME Group.

Dow Jones

The Dow Jones Industrial Average.

A price-weighted index of 30 large US blue-chip companies, tracking the health of major established industry.

Calculated and maintained by S&P Dow Jones Indices.

Effective Fed Funds Rate

The actual (as opposed to targeted) overnight rate banks charge each other for reserves.

It shows where the Fed Funds rate is really trading within the FOMC's announced target range, compared here to the Fed's own longer-run 'neutral rate' estimate.

Calculated and published daily by the Federal Reserve Bank of New York.

Ethereum

The second-largest cryptocurrency by market value.

Unlike Bitcoin, its blockchain runs programmable 'smart contracts,' making it the base layer for most decentralized finance (DeFi) applications.

Tracked via public blockchain data; price shown here reflects trading on major exchanges via Yahoo Finance.

Euro

The official currency of the eurozone - the 20 European Union member states that have adopted it, including Germany, France, Italy, and Spain.

Exchange rate shown here is against the US dollar.

Published daily by the Federal Reserve (H.10 foreign exchange release).

Euro Stoxx 50

An index of 50 large-cap companies spanning multiple eurozone countries.

Tracks the broader eurozone blue-chip market as a whole, rather than any single country's exchange.

Calculated and maintained by STOXX, a Deutsche Börse Group company.

Fed Funds Rate

The interest rate banks charge each other for overnight loans of reserves.

It's the base rate the Federal Reserve uses to influence borrowing costs throughout the economy - mortgages, credit cards, and business loans all key off it.

The target range is set by the Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC); the actual effective rate is calculated and published daily by the Federal Reserve Bank of New York.

Calculated and maintained by FTSE Russell in partnership with Borsa Italiana.

Gold

High-Yield Credit Spread

The extra yield 'junk' (below-investment-grade) corporate bonds pay over comparable Treasuries.

It measures how much extra compensation investors demand for credit risk; under ~3.5% is calm, 5%+ signals building stress, 8%+ is crisis territory.

Calculated by ICE Data Indices and published via the Federal Reserve's FRED database.

The share of single-family mortgages 30+ days past due on payments.

Natl Avg 12-Mo CD (FDIC)

Natl Avg Credit Card Rate

Natl Avg Money Market (FDIC)

The national average interest rate paid on money market deposit accounts.

It measures typical bank money-market yields, distinct from money market mutual funds.

Published monthly by the FDIC.

Natl Avg Savings (FDIC)

The national average interest rate paid on standard savings accounts.

It measures what a typical bank (not the high-yield outliers) pays depositors.

Published monthly by the FDIC.

Overbought (RSI≥70)

A Relative Strength Index reading of 70 or above.

RSI measures how fast and how far a stock's price has moved recently; a high reading means it has been bought up hard and fast and is sometimes due for a pullback.

RSI is a technical indicator calculated directly from each stock's own price history, not published by any government or index agency.

Oversold (RSI≤30)

A Relative Strength Index reading of 30 or below.

RSI measures how fast and how far a stock's price has moved recently; a low reading means it has sold off hard and fast and is sometimes due for a bounce.

RSI is a technical indicator calculated directly from each stock's own price history, not published by any government or index agency.

Palladium

Palladium, price per troy ounce.

Used primarily in catalytic converters for gasoline engines, making it closely tied to auto production and emissions regulations.

Priced and traded on the NYMEX exchange, operated by CME Group.

Platinum

Platinum, price per troy ounce.

Used heavily in catalytic converters and jewelry, so it responds to both industrial (especially auto) demand and precious-metal investment demand.

Polkadot

PPI (Producer Prices)

The Producer Price Index, year-over-year change.

It measures how much prices businesses receive for their output rose over the past 12 months, and often leads consumer inflation since higher input costs get passed downstream.

Calculated and published monthly by the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS).

RBOB Gasoline

The wholesale gasoline benchmark used to price fuel before retail markup and taxes - a leading indicator for pump prices.

Real GDP Growth (YoY)

It measures the total growth of everything the US economy produces, stripped of price changes so it reflects actual output growth.

Calculated and published quarterly by the Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA).

Retail Sales
